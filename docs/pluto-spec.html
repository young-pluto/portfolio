04

The grid, and the aspect-ratio problem

Below is the real thing: the three clips I cut from your recordings this afternoon, running in a three-column grid. Use the buttons to see why the crop matters.

Crop

1:1 keeps 46% of the phone screen — the middle band, which is where the content always is.

Why this was nearly a problem

Your recordings are 1284 × 2778 — an iPhone screen, 1 : 2.16. Instagram posts are 1:1 or 4:5. Letterboxing a phone screen into 4:5 spends 21% of the frame width on empty bars down each side, and into 1:1 it spends more than half the frame. It looks like a mistake.

Centre-cropping instead works, because a phone screen puts its content in the middle band and its chrome at the edges. The three crops keep 46%, 58% and 69% of the screen height respectively, and in every case the part that survives is the part that matters.

Decision. Encode one master per clip at native aspect, 720px wide, and crop at render time with object-fit: cover plus a per-post object-position. One encode serves the grid, the post and the reel, and the framing of any individual post can be nudged later without re-encoding anything.

The notes thread, using Ball4Life's real content

@architecture

Why one ledger writer? All revenue — website and admin alike — enters the books through a single idempotent writer, so every dashboard KPI and every P&L export derives from one source of truth.

@reliability

Dual payment verification. A Cashfree webhook and a client-side polling fallback race each other; an atomic Firebase transaction guarantees the winner is processed exactly once.

@tradeoff

Schema debt compounds. The flat 48-slot schema kept the v1→v2 migration data-compatible, but a fourth bookable day or a second court means reworking the core model. The mapping layer was the bridge, not the destination.

@next

Own the critical path. A legacy Gen-1 Cloud Function still handles slot locking and notifications over fire-and-forget HTTP. Folding it into the server actions removes the hop and the silent-failure mode.

Four fields from the existing project record — solution, highlights, learnings, impactMetric — become four notes. The content model barely moves; only the presentation does. And a candidate who writes @tradeoff and @next notes about his own work reads as someone with judgement, which is the actual thing being assessed.

07

The ring that has to be live

One story ring sits apart from the highlights, with the gradient border Instagram reserves for unseen stories and a BUILDING label. Three or four cards: what you are working on this month, a screenshot, a half-finished thought. Dated, so a stale one is visibly stale.

This is the only part of the site that changes between deploys, and it is what stops the portfolio reading as a thing you last touched in September. It is also the entire justification for a backend — see section 11.

If the newest card is more than six weeks old the ring renders grey rather than gradient, and the label reads the date instead of BUILDING. An honestly stale ring beats a ring that lies about being live.

10

Navigation, URLs and the splash

The splash, and when it must not play

Black, the handle, then the profile — 700ms, as you wanted. But it plays once per session, and never on a deep link. Someone opening /p/ball4life from a Slack message is already looking for one specific thing; making them watch an animation first is the difference between a clever opening and an annoying one. It is also skipped entirely under prefers-reduced-motion.

The unglamorous requirement

Every caption, note and role description ships as real text in the initial HTML, every video carries a poster frame, and every grid tile is a real <a>. A recruiter will paste this URL into an applicant tracking system, or open it on hotel wifi, or share it to someone who reads it with the images off. A single-page app that renders its content only after JavaScript fails all three. This is a constraint on the build, not a polish item at the end.

11

Three decisions I made for you

You did not answer these, so I have specced an answer for each and flagged it. Overrule any of them and I will rework the affected sections before anything gets built.

Backend: static content, except the story ring

Posts, highlights and employment live in a content module beside the code, the way the current project array does. Only the BUILDING ring reads from Firestore.

Why: the whole trick is the page feeling as immediate as an app. A Firestore round-trip on first paint buys a loading state on the one screen that cannot afford one. The story ring is the only content that genuinely needs to change without a deploy — so it is the only content that pays for a network call.

Fidelity: faithful chrome, content does the reveal

Instagram's real navigation, real grid, real story viewer, real icons. No renamed tabs — the tabs stay Posts, Reels and Tagged, because renaming them to "Builds · Experiments · Experience" breaks the illusion in the first two seconds, which is the opposite of what it is for.

Why: this is the direction you argued for yourself — keep the UI and let the content reveal it. The reveal only lands if the interface is committed enough to be mistaken for the real thing first.

Desktop: build Instagram's actual desktop layout

Wide profile header, centred grid, posts open as a modal over a dimmed profile with the caption in a right-hand column. Not a phone-width column floating on a large screen.

Why: recruiters open links on desktop. Instagram's desktop web is a real design we can borrow honestly, and a 400px column centred on a 27-inch monitor reads as an unfinished mobile site rather than a deliberate choice.

Still genuinely yours to decide

The handle and the domain

This spec assumes youngpluto throughout — your example, and close to your GitHub young-pluto. It becomes the URL, the browser tab (Instagram · @youngpluto), and the entire conceit, so it is worth deciding deliberately rather than inheriting from a draft. Does it live at builtbyapoorv.in, or somewhere new?

What replaces the current site

The site at builtbyapoorv.in is genuinely good — the architecture diagrams and the honest learnings are rarer than anything in this spec. Does this become the front door with that one click away, or a second site at its own address? I would keep the current one alive at /classic either way: some readers want the dense version, and it costs nothing to leave it standing.
